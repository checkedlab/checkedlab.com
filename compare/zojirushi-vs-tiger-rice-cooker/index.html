Zojirushi vs Tiger: which to buy

Zojirushi lists the NS-ZCC10 at up to 5.5 cups (1.0 liter) and 680 watts, made in Japan. Tiger lists the JBV-A10U as a 5.5-cup cooker, 1 liter and 672 watts, made in China. The cabinets are close: 10-1/8 by 13 by 8-1/8 inches for Zojirushi, and 10.6 by 13.9 by 8.4 inches for the JBV-A10U.

Choose the Zojirushi if

  • You want up to 5.5 cups at 680 watts, made in Japan.
  • You want the Neuro Fuzzy menu list, including sushi, sweet, semi-brown, and quick.
  • You have 8-1/8 inches of height and you want the 10-1/8 by 13 inch footprint.

  • You want the cooker Tiger lists as made in China, at about 5.7 lb.

How to read this

The table keeps a row only when both makers print that label. The Source link opens the sentence the number came from. A figure only one maker prints sits under this fold. The recall line is the CPSC and Health Canada check dated 2026-11-13 (UTC).

The Zojirushi NS-ZCC10 is the 5.5-cup Neuro Fuzzy cooker: up to 5.5 cups or 1.0 liter, 10-1/8 by 13 by 8-1/8 inches, 120 volts and 680 watts, made in Japan. Tiger’s US kitchen list calls the JBV-A10U a 5.5-cup cooker. The JBV product spec table says 1 L (34 oz), 672 watts, 120 V, 60 Hz, and a cabinet of 10.6 by 13.9 by 8.4 inches.

Origin is the clean split on the spec tables: Japan for the NS-ZCC10, China for the JBV-A10U. Watts are close, 680 against 672. The menu lists are not the same length.

Zojirushi vs Tiger at the 5.5-cup size

These two are the same class of cooker: a 5.5-cup, about 1-liter microcomputer rice cooker, not an induction cooker and not a 10-cup pot. Zojirushi measures capacity in its own cup, about 6.1 fl oz or 180 mL, using raw short-grain white rice, and says other grains may vary. Tiger’s kitchen-products list puts JBV-A10U under Capacity 5.5 Cups, with dimensions that match the product spec table. The product table itself prints Capacity as 1 L and 34 oz, electric rating 672 W, and heating system Micom + Heating.

Footprints are close enough that a shelf which fits one may fit the other, and close enough that you should still measure. Zojirushi prints 10-1/8 by 13 by 8-1/8 inches. Tiger prints about 10.6 by 13.9 by 8.4 inches, and about 5.7 lb (2.6 kg). Zojirushi’s spec block on the store page does not print a weight. Tiger’s producing country is China. Zojirushi’s is Japan, and the store page says the cooker is cETLus listed.

A smaller Tiger is easy to confuse with this one. Tiger’s US homepage lists a Micom Rice Cooker JBS-A55U released on 21 April 2026. Tiger’s USA product page for the JBS-A55U says plain-rice capacity of 0.5 to 3 cups, 0.54 L, 360 watts, about 9.7 by 10.9 by 7.6 inches, and Japan as the producing country. That is a 3-cup cooker. It is not the JBV-A10U in this comparison.

Menus, keep warm, and what is in the box

Zojirushi’s menu line is the longer one: white rice on regular, sushi, softer, or harder, plus mixed, porridge, sweet, semi-brown, brown, rinse-free, and quick. The same page says automatic keep warm, extended keep warm, and a reheating cycle, a delay timer with two settings, a spherical inner pan, and a retractable power cord. It does not print a maximum keep-warm hour count in the block opened here.

Tiger’s JBV-A page names four menus: plain, brown, slow cooking, and synchro-cooking. It says keep warm for up to 12 hours. The included accessories are a tacook plate, a spatula, a rice measuring cup, and a recipe cookbook. Tiger says the plate cooks rice and a side dish together without the flavors mixing. The inner pot is described as aluminum with a fluorine coating. The warranty block says one year from the original purchase for Tiger electric products bought in the United States and Canada, for 120 V products.

Zojirushi’s store page links a limited warranty for electrical products and does not print the year count in the section opened here, so this table does not invent one. There is also an NS-ZCC18 on the same Zojirushi page, at up to 10 cups, 1.8 liters, and 1,000 watts. This comparison is the NS-ZCC10 only.

How to choose the 5.5-cup cooker

Pick the Zojirushi if you want 680 watts, made in Japan, the longer menu list, and a cabinet of 10-1/8 by 13 by 8-1/8 inches. Pick the Tiger JBV-A10U if you want 672 watts, the tacook plate, a stated 12-hour keep warm, and you accept China as the producing country on that spec table.

When the Zojirushi store page was opened, the NS-ZCC10 showed out of stock. Tiger’s kitchen list still presents the JBV-A series as a current cooker. Availability changes. Match NS-ZCC10, not NS-ZCC18, and match JBV-A10U, not JBV-A18U and not the 3-cup JBS-A55U. The worth-it note for the NS-ZCC10 uses these same Zojirushi figures.

Questions

Are the Zojirushi NS-ZCC10 and the Tiger JBV-A10U the same size?

Both are in the 5.5-cup class. Zojirushi says up to 5.5 cups or 1.0 liter. Tiger’s product table says 1 L (34 oz), and Tiger’s kitchen list calls the JBV-A10U a 5.5-cup cooker.

Which uses more watts?

Zojirushi lists 680 watts at 120 volts. Tiger lists 672 watts at 120 V and 60 Hz.

Where does each maker say the cooker is made?

Zojirushi says made in Japan. Tiger’s JBV-A10U spec table says the producing country is China. The newer JBS-A55U, a 3-cup cooker, is listed as made in Japan and is not this comparison.

What menus does each one name?

Zojirushi names white (regular, sushi, softer, or harder), mixed, porridge, sweet, semi-brown, brown, rinse-free, and quick. Tiger’s JBV-A page names plain, brown, slow cooking, and synchro-cooking, plus keep warm up to 12 hours.

Is the Tiger JBS-A55U the same cooker?

No. Tiger’s USA page for the JBS-A55U says 0.5 to 3 cups, 0.54 L, and 360 watts. The homepage dates that release to 21 April 2026. The 5.5-cup model in this comparison is the JBV-A10U.
