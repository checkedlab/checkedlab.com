Turkey temperatures and thermometers

USDA FSIS temperatures for turkey, stuffing, thawing, and leftovers, plus the CPSC notice for specific iDevices thermometer probes.

What to read on the label

  • USDA’s Turkey Basics: Safe Cooking says to use a food thermometer and to reach a safe minimum internal temperature of 165 °F in the innermost part of the thigh and wing and the thickest part of the breast. It says the center of the stuffing must also reach 165 °F. It says that for optimum safety, cook stuffing in a casserole. It says to set the oven no lower than 325 °F, to start with a completely thawed turkey held at a refrigerator temperature of 40 °F or below, and to place the turkey breast-side up on a flat wire rack in a shallow roasting pan 2 to 2 1/2 inches deep. It says a dark roasting pan cooks faster than a shiny one. It says to let the bird stand 20 minutes before removing the stuffing and carving.
  • USDA’s Let’s Talk Turkey says the three safe thawing methods are the refrigerator, cold water, and the microwave. It says refrigerator thawing is about 24 hours for every 4 to 5 pounds at 40 °F or below, that the wrapper stays on, and that a thawed turkey can remain in the refrigerator 1 to 2 days. It says a turkey thawed in the refrigerator may be refrozen. It says cold-water thawing is about 30 minutes per pound, in a wrap that does not leak, with the water changed every 30 minutes, and that the turkey is cooked immediately and not refrozen. It says a microwave-thawed turkey is cooked immediately and is not refrozen or refrigerated after thawing. It says to remove the giblets after thawing. It says a pop-up indicator is not a substitute for checking the thigh, wing, and thickest part of the breast with a food thermometer.
  • The same USDA page says to discard turkey, stuffing, and gravy left out longer than 2 hours, or 1 hour when the temperature is above 90 °F. It says to divide leftovers into smaller portions and refrigerate or freeze them in covered shallow containers. It says to use refrigerated turkey, stuffing, and gravy within 3 to 4 days. It says to reheat turkey to 165 °F, checked with a food thermometer, and to set the oven no lower than 325 °F when reheating in the oven. It says a turkey cooked from the frozen state takes at least 50 percent longer than the time for a fully thawed turkey, and that the giblet packages are removed during cooking.

How to search a recall

For a purchase in the United States, search the brand and the model on CPSC Recalls. For a purchase in Canada, search the same words on Health Canada recalls and safety alerts. Open the notice and match the model number on the unit. A family name on the box can point at a different model.

A thermometer notice names a probe, a date range, or a crimp pattern. The iDevices notice below does not name every food thermometer. Read the name on the probe and on the package, then search that name on CPSC Recalls and on Health Canada recalls and safety alerts.

Patterns in official notices

Each pattern below is one official page. The sentence is what that page says.

  • USDA FSIS: Turkey Basics: Safe Cooking. The page says a food thermometer should be used to ensure a safe minimum internal temperature of 165 °F has been reached. It says to check the innermost part of the thigh and wing and the thickest part of the breast, and to check that the center of the stuffing also reaches 165 °F. It says many variables change the roasting time, including a partially frozen turkey, a stuffed turkey, an oven that heats unevenly, a dark pan, the depth of the pan, a foil tent, a lid, an oven bag, and the rack position. It says the standing time of 20 minutes is for quality, so the juices set and the turkey carves more easily.
  • USDA FSIS: Let’s Talk Turkey: thawing, leftovers, and reheating. The page says to thaw only in the refrigerator, in cold water, or in the microwave, with the times and the cook-immediately rules printed there. It says not to thaw a frozen pre-stuffed turkey that bears a USDA or state inspection mark; cook it from the frozen state and follow the package. It says leftovers of turkey, stuffing, and gravy left out longer than 2 hours, or 1 hour above 90 °F, should be discarded. It says refrigerated leftovers of those foods are used within 3 to 4 days. It says reheated turkey should reach 165 °F on a food thermometer. The page was last updated November 17, 2023.
  • CPSC: iDevices recalls temperature probes. The notice is dated September 3, 2014. It says the plastic insulator inside the stainless steel probe is not heat resistant and can melt and fall into food, posing an ingestion hazard. It covers all Pro Ambient Temperature Probes and Pro Meat Probes manufactured from May 2014 through June 2014, sold as accessories for the iGrill, iGrill2, iGrillmini, Kitchen Thermometer, and Kitchen Thermometer mini, and the meat probe also sold with the iGrill2 set. It says recalled probes have only two crimps where the probe tube meets the braided cable. It says about 48,500 were sold in the United States and 510 in Canada. It says the firm had 11 reports of the probe overheating and the insulator melting, and no reported injuries. It tells owners to stop using the recalled probes and contact iDevices for a free replacement.

Questions

What temperature does USDA FSIS name for turkey?

Turkey Basics: Safe Cooking says 165 °F in the innermost part of the thigh and wing and the thickest part of the breast, and 165 °F in the center of the stuffing. Let’s Talk Turkey says a pop-up indicator is still checked with a food thermometer in those same places. The oven temperature on both pages is no lower than 325 °F.

How does USDA say to thaw a turkey?

Let’s Talk Turkey says the refrigerator, cold water, or the microwave. Refrigerator thawing is about 24 hours for every 4 to 5 pounds. Cold-water thawing is about 30 minutes per pound, with the water changed every 30 minutes, and the turkey is cooked immediately. A microwave-thawed turkey is cooked immediately and is not refrigerated after thawing.

Does the iDevices notice cover every meat thermometer?

No. The September 3, 2014 CPSC notice names Pro Ambient Temperature Probes and Pro Meat Probes made from May 2014 through June 2014, and it describes two crimps at the cable. Search the brand and model of the thermometer you have on CPSC Recalls and on Health Canada recalls and safety alerts.
