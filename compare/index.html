Spec comparisons, from the maker pages

Pairs of catalogue models. Each page repeats spec lines that both maker pages share, with a source link and a dated recall line.

How to read a spec comparison

A comparison on this site is a narrow table, not a verdict. It repeats spec lines that both maker pages share, and it links the maker page from the cell. A label that appears on only one card stays on that card. That rule is the whole method. It keeps a pascal figure from sitting in the same row as an air-watt figure, and it keeps a CADR from sitting beside a card that never prints CADR. How we check describes the dated recall line that sits under the table. The date is the day the catalogue match was recorded.

Read the table for the decision you actually have. If you are choosing between two jump starters, the useful rows are peak current, the gas and diesel engine limits, weight, and the enclosure rating, because both NOCO pages print those labels. The GB40 page states 1,000 A peak, gas engines up to 6 L, diesel engines up to 3 L, and 2.4 lb. The GB50 page states 1,500 A, gas up to 7 L, diesel up to 4.5 L, and 2.7 lb. Both state IP65. Peak current is the figure those pages print.

The same rule explains the air purifier pairs. Coway Mighty and Levoit Core 300S-P share dimensions and weight. Coway states 16.8 × 18.3 × 9.6 in and 12.3 lb. Levoit states 8.7 × 8.7 × 14.2 in and 7.48 lb. CADR, room coverage, and power stay on the Coway card. Noise and rated power stay on the Levoit card. Honeywell HPA300 and Coway Airmega 250 are a different pair, and Levoit Core 600S and Winix 5500-2 are another. Do not drag a CADR from one pair onto a model that is not in that pair.

Room size is the easiest number to misuse, including across two honest cards. The Core 600S page states 2,909 sq ft at 1 air change per hour and 606 sq ft at 4.8. The Coway Airmega 250 page states 465 sq ft at 4 air changes per hour and 1,860 sq ft at 1 air change per hour. The Winix 5500-2 manual states an AHAM verified 360 sq ft. Those sentences are not one scale. When a comparison leaves room size out, it is because the two pages did not print the same label. Open both cards and read the rate attached to the square footage.

Vacuum pairs need the same care with units. Dyson V15 Detect Absolute and Dyson V8 can share weight and run time because both Dyson pages print them. The V8 page states 5.58 lb and a 40 minute maximum. The V15 page states 6.83 lb and up to 60 minutes. V15 and Shark IP3251 put a 6.83 lb cordless next to an 18.47 lb cordless whose run time is stated up to 70 minutes at the hand vacuum in ECO mode. Shark AV2501S and AV2820AE share a 5.7 in cleaning path and state different weights, voltages, and wattages. Do not convert pascals on a robot card into air watts on a Dyson card. The table will not do that conversion either.

Every comparison also repeats the recall line for each model, dated with the catalogue check. Search the model on CPSC Recalls for a US purchase and on Health Canada recalls and safety alerts for a Canadian purchase. The feeds behind the catalogue match are CPSC SaferProducts and the Health Canada open dataset. A clear line on the left-hand model does not cover the right-hand model. The category hubs linked under each pair hold the rest of the cards.

Questions

Why is a spec missing from the table?

Because only one of the two maker pages prints that label. CADR on the Coway Mighty card stays off the Mighty and Core 300S-P table because the Levoit card does not use that label. The missing row is still on the model page, with its source link.

Can I compare pascals on a robot with air watts on a Dyson?

No. They are different units, and the tables do not convert one into the other. Read the pascal line on the robot card and the air-watt line on the Dyson card as separate sentences.

Which room-size number should I keep?

Keep the number and the air-change rate that the maker attached to it. The Core 600S page states both 2,909 sq ft at 1 air change per hour and 606 sq ft at 4.8. The larger figure and the smaller figure answer different questions.

Does the comparison include a recall check?

Yes. Each model repeats the catalogue sentence for the US match and the Canadian match, dated with the day that match was recorded. Search the same model on CPSC Recalls or Health Canada recalls and safety alerts if you want the official search page open beside it.

Where do the cell values come from?

From the maker page linked in that cell. The sentence above the table on each comparison says so. A value is not an average of the two models and not a measurement added by this catalogue.
