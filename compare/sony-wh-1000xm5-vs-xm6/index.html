Sony XM5 vs XM6: which to buy

Music playback matches: Sony lists a maximum of 30 hours with noise cancelling on, 40 hours with it off, and a charge of about 3.5 hours for both. The XM5 weighs about 8.82 oz and uses Bluetooth 5.2. The XM6 weighs about 8.96 oz, uses Bluetooth 5.3, and Sony says its QN3 processor optimizes 12 microphones. Choose the XM5 for the lower weight and the longer call-time line with noise cancelling off. Choose the XM6 for the newer processor, LC3, and Bluetooth 5.3.

Choose the XM5 if

  • You want the lower weight, about 8.82 oz.
  • You want Bluetooth 5.2 and call time up to 32 hours with noise cancelling off.
  • You want the same 30-hour and 40-hour music lines without the XM6 processor.

Choose the XM6 if

  • You want the QN3 processor and 12 microphones.
  • You want Bluetooth 5.3 and the LC3 codec on the spec list.
  • You can accept about 8.96 oz and a 28-hour call line with noise cancelling off.

How to read this

The table keeps a row only when both makers print that label. The Source link opens the sentence the number came from. A figure only one maker prints sits under this fold. The recall line is the CPSC and Health Canada check dated 2026-11-13 (UTC).

Sony’s specifications pages list the same music-playback line for both: max 30 hours with noise cancelling on and max 40 hours with it off. Both charge in about 3.5 hours over USB. Weight is about 8.82 oz for the XM5 and about 8.96 oz for the XM6.

The split is the radio and the processor. The XM5 spec page says Bluetooth 5.2 and codecs SBC, AAC, and LDAC. The XM6 spec page says Bluetooth 5.3 and adds LC3. Call time with noise cancelling off is max 32 hours on the XM5 list and max 28 hours on the XM6 list. With noise cancelling on, both say max 24 hours.

Sony XM5 vs XM6 battery and weight

If you are buying on the music-hour line alone, these two specification pages do not separate the headphones. Both say max 30 hours with noise cancelling on and max 40 hours with it off, and both say a charge time of about 3.5 hours by USB. Sony’s XM6 product notes say actual battery life varies with settings, conditions, and age, and that Sony does not guarantee battery lifespan. The XM5 product notes say the same kind of thing: performance varies, and capacity decreases over time.

Weight is a small gap, and it favors the XM5: about 8.82 oz against about 8.96 oz. Both are closed, over-ear headphones with a neodymium magnet, a detachable single-sided cord of about 3.94 ft, a gold-plated L-shaped mini plug, and passive operation. Both spec pages say ambient sound and quick attention are yes. Multipoint is on both product pages, and the XM6 spec page also prints multipoint as yes.

The processor, the codecs, and a driver line to read twice

Sony’s XM6 product page says the HD Noise Canceling Processor QN3 is 7 times faster than the QN1 in the WH-1000XM5, and that it optimizes 12 microphones in real time. The same page says an Adaptive NC Optimizer adjusts to outside noise, air pressure, and how you wear the headphones, including with a hat or glasses. The XM5 specifications page does not print a microphone count. The 12-microphone sentence and the QN1 comparison live on the XM6 page.

Bluetooth is version 5.2 on the XM5 spec page and version 5.3 on the XM6 spec page. Codecs are SBC, AAC, and LDAC for the XM5. The XM6 list adds LC3. Frequency response on the XM6 page is 4–40,000 Hz (JEITA) for both the general line and active operation. The XM5 page says 4 Hz–40,000 Hz (JEITA). Effective range is about 32.81 ft on the XM6 page and 32.81 ft on the XM5 page.

Do not treat the driver line as a clean size comparison. The XM5 specifications page prints a driver unit of 11.82 in. The XM6 specifications page prints 1.18 in. Those two official lines are not a sensible pair of measurements for the same kind of driver, so this table leaves driver size out. Impedance is the same shape on both lists: 48 ohm at 1 kHz with the cable and the unit on, and 16 ohm at 1 kHz with the unit off. Sensitivity is 102 dB and 100 dB on the XM5 list, and 103 dB/mW and 102 dB/mW on the XM6 list, each with the unit on and then off.

What is in the box, and which model name to match

Both spec pages list a carrying case, a connection cable, and a USB cable. The XM6 list also names a warranty card and a reference guide. The XM5 list stops at the case and the two cables. The XM6 product page says earpads can be replaced by hand, and a footnote says the adapter is not included. The XM5 product notes also say the adapter is not included. Colors on the XM6 page’s own question list are Black, Midnight Blue, Platinum Silver, Sand Pink, and Sandstone, and Sony says color availability can vary by country.

Pick the XM5 if you want about 8.82 oz, Bluetooth 5.2, SBC/AAC/LDAC, and the 32-hour call line with noise cancelling off. Pick the XM6 if you want the QN3 processor, 12 microphones, Bluetooth 5.3, and LC3, and the 8.96 oz weight is acceptable. Match the model code WH-1000XM5 or WH-1000XM6 on the box. A Hello Kitty edition is a different XM6 listing on Sony’s site, with its own color name. The Bose QuietComfort Ultra comparison uses the XM5 card and does not describe the XM6.

Questions

Does the Sony XM6 last longer than the XM5?

Not on the music-playback line. Both specification pages say max 30 hours with noise cancelling on and max 40 hours with it off. Call time with noise cancelling off is longer on the XM5 list: max 32 hours, against max 28 hours for the XM6.

Which one weighs less?

Sony lists the XM5 at about 8.82 oz and the XM6 at about 8.96 oz.

What is the noise-cancelling difference Sony states?

The XM6 product page says the QN3 processor is 7 times faster than the QN1 in the WH-1000XM5 and that it optimizes 12 microphones. The XM5 specifications page does not print a microphone count.

Do both charge in the same time?

Yes. Both specification pages say a battery charge time of about 3.5 hours, by USB.

Which Bluetooth version and codecs does each list?

The XM5 spec page says Bluetooth 5.2 and SBC, AAC, and LDAC. The XM6 spec page says Bluetooth 5.3 and SBC, AAC, LDAC, and LC3. Both product pages list multipoint.
