Mr Heater Buddy vs Big Buddy: which to buy

The Portable Buddy MH9BX is 4,000 or 9,000 BTU and up to 225 sq ft, from one 1 lb propane cylinder. The Big Buddy MH18B is 4,000, 9,000, or 18,000 BTU and up to 450 sq ft, from two 1 lb cylinders. Both standard listings are marked indoor safe, with Massachusetts and Canada restrictions, and both have a tip-over shutoff and an oxygen depletion sensor.

Choose the Buddy if

  • You want 4,000 or 9,000 BTU and up to 225 sq ft.
  • You want one 1 lb cylinder and a 3-hour run at the maximum setting.
  • You want the smaller heater, with a spec-table weight of 10.6 and no unit named.
See the model

Choose the Big Buddy if

  • You want 18,000 BTU and up to 450 sq ft.
  • You can run two 1 lb cylinders, which the high setting needs.
  • You want hi, medium, and low, and you can tell the indoor listing from the Massachusetts and Canada version.

How to read this

The table keeps a row only when both makers print that label. The Source link opens the sentence the number came from. A figure only one maker prints sits under this fold. The recall line is the CPSC and Health Canada check dated 2026-11-13 (UTC).

Mr. Heater’s Portable Buddy page is model MH9BX: 4,000 or 9,000 BTU per hour, up to 225 sq ft, one 1 lb cylinder, hi and low, piezo ignition, an oxygen depletion sensor, and a tip-over shutoff. The spec table says 3 hours at the maximum BTU and 6 hours at the minimum.

The Big Buddy page is model MH18B: 4,000, 9,000, or 18,000 BTU, up to 450 sq ft, two 1 lb cylinders, and hi, med, and low. Both standard pages mark indoor safe as yes and list Massachusetts and Canada as restrictions. A separate Big Buddy page is the Massachusetts and Canada version, and that page marks indoor safe as no.

Mr Heater Buddy vs Big Buddy heat, area, and fuel

The Portable Buddy is the one-cylinder heater. Mr. Heater says it connects to a 1 lb cylinder, can connect to a 20 lb cylinder with an optional hose and filter, and has a swivel regulator and a fold-down handle. Heat is 4,000 or 9,000 BTU. The area line is up to 225 sq ft. The spec table also prints fuel use of 0.044 gallons per hour at 4,000 BTU and 0.099 gallons per hour at 9,000 BTU, a maximum elevation of 7000 ft, length 14.25, width 9, and height 15. Those length lines do not name inches. Weight prints as 10.6, also with no unit.

The Big Buddy is the two-cylinder heater. Heat is 4,000, 9,000, or 18,000 BTU. The area line is up to 450 sq ft. The spec table says the tank is two 1 lb propane cylinders, elevation 7,000 feet, weight 17.1, length 19, and width 12, again without naming the unit on the weight and the length. Ignition is piezo. The oxygen depletion sensor and the tip-over switch are yes. A hose and regulator are sold separately. Mr. Heater’s Buddy family notes say the 18,000 BTU high setting needs two 1 lb bottles, or a larger remote tank. One small bottle is not enough vapor for that high setting, and the tank can frost.

Warranty is a 3 year limited warranty on both spec tables. Thermostat optional is no on both. Fuel is propane on both. The use lists overlap: tents, campers, workshops, job sites, garages, patios, hunting blinds, ice-fishing shanties, and similar spots. The Big Buddy page also says cabins in the description.

Indoor-safe marks, and two different MH18B listings

The Portable Buddy page marks indoor safe as yes, and it lists state restrictions for Massachusetts and country restrictions for Canada. The standard Big Buddy page does the same: indoor safe yes, restricted in Massachusetts and Canada. Mr. Heater also sells a Big Buddy page titled as the Massachusetts and Canada version. That page says the heater is for recreational enclosures, construction sites, and outdoors, and its spec table marks indoor safe as no. If you are buying for Massachusetts or Canada, open that version and read the indoor-safe line before you treat the standard page as the one you can use there.

Run time is messier on the Portable Buddy page than the 3-hour line suggests. The spec table says 3 hours at maximum BTU and 6 hours at minimum BTU. A bullet on the same page says 225 sq ft of coverage for up to 5.4 hours. Those are both on the page. Use the table when you want the max-BTU and min-BTU pair, and do not ignore the 5.4-hour bullet. The Big Buddy spec table opened here does not print a run-time row. A bullet says 450 sq ft for up to 11 hours with dual tanks. There is no second official number next to it on that table.

Mr. Heater also says the fan has been removed on some Big Buddy MH18B units because of import tariffs, and that the Portable Buddy tip-over switch may now be audible when the heater is shaken. Those notes are on the product pages. A missing fan on one MH18B is not a different BTU rating. It is a reason to read the unit in front of you against the page.

How to choose, then what to confirm

Pick the Portable Buddy if you want 4,000 or 9,000 BTU, up to 225 sq ft, one 1 lb cylinder, and the 3-hour maximum run line in the spec table. Pick the Big Buddy if you want up to 18,000 BTU, up to 450 sq ft, and you can supply two 1 lb cylinders for the high setting. Both standard listings say indoor safe yes, with the Massachusetts and Canada restriction. The Massachusetts and Canada Big Buddy listing says indoor safe no.

Match MH9BX or MH18B on the heater, not the Buddy family name alone. Mr. Heater also sells a Little Buddy and a Hunting Buddy. This comparison is only MH9BX and MH18B. The space-heater safety guide is the place for the label checks and the official recall searches. It does not replace the BTU lines above. When these pages were opened, the Portable Buddy showed available and the standard Big Buddy showed out of stock. That can change.

Questions

How many BTU does each Buddy list?

The Portable Buddy MH9BX lists 4,000 or 9,000 BTU per hour. The Big Buddy MH18B lists 4,000, 9,000, or 18,000 BTU per hour.

How much area does Mr. Heater name?

Up to 225 sq ft for the MH9BX. Up to 450 sq ft for the MH18B.

How many propane cylinders does each one use?

The MH9BX runs on one 1 lb cylinder. The MH18B connects to two 1 lb cylinders. Mr. Heater’s Buddy notes say the 18,000 BTU setting needs both bottles, or a larger remote tank.

Are they marked indoor safe?

The standard MH9BX page and the standard MH18B page both mark indoor safe as yes, and both list Massachusetts and Canada restrictions. The separate Massachusetts and Canada MH18B page marks indoor safe as no.

What run time does the Portable Buddy table print?

3 hours at the maximum BTU and 6 hours at the minimum. A bullet on the same page also says up to 5.4 hours. The Big Buddy table does not print a run-time row. A bullet there says up to 11 hours with dual tanks.
