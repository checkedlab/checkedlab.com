High-powered magnets

What CPSC and Health Canada say about small powerful magnet sets, and how that differs from a toy covered by the toy standard.

What to read on the label

  • Read whether the set is sold as a toy for a child under 14 or as a novelty set for entertainment, jewelry, or stress relief. CPSC magnet guidance says 16 CFR part 1262 applies to subject magnet products manufactured after October 21, 2022. If a loose or separable magnet fits entirely in the small parts cylinder, its flux index must be less than 50 kG2 mm2.
  • The same page says toys subject to 16 CFR part 1250, which incorporates ASTM F963, are exempt from the magnet rule. The September 7, 2022 CPSC release says the rule does not apply to toys for children under 14 because the toy standard already covers them.
  • Health Canada’s advice on magnetic toys says the Toys Regulations limit the strength and size of magnets in toys, and says not to buy novelty magnet sets marketed for adults. It says those sets are illegal to sell in Canada if the powerful magnets are small enough to be swallowed.

How to search a recall

For a purchase in the United States, search the brand and the model on CPSC Recalls. For a purchase in Canada, search the same words on Health Canada recalls and safety alerts. Open the notice and match the model number on the unit. A family name on the box can point at a different model.

A magnetic building set for a child and a loose magnet set sold for adults are different products. Read the age label and the product name on the box, then search that name.

Patterns in official notices

Each pattern below is one official page. The sentence is what that page says.

  • CPSC: Safety Standard for Magnets. The page says the final rule, 87 FR 57756, is the Safety Standard for Magnets at 16 CFR part 1262. It says subject products are designed, marketed, or intended for entertainment, jewelry including children’s jewelry, mental stimulation, stress relief, or a combination of those. It says every loose or separable magnet that fits in the small parts cylinder must have a flux index less than 50 kG2 mm2. It says toys subject to 16 CFR part 1250 are exempt.
  • CPSC: CPSC approves the magnet standard. The September 7, 2022 release says swallowed high-powered magnets can attract through internal tissue and can perforate, twist, or block the intestines. It says CPSC estimates 26,600 magnet ingestions were treated in hospital emergency rooms from 2010 through 2021, and that CPSC is aware of seven deaths involving ingestion of hazardous magnets, including two outside the United States. It urges owners of these magnets and magnet sets to discard them.
  • Health Canada: Small powerful magnets under the Canada Consumer Product Safety Act. The assessment says paragraphs 7(a) and 8(a) of the Canada Consumer Product Safety Act prohibit manufacture, importation, advertisement, or sale of a consumer product that is a danger to human health or safety. It says novelty magnet sets and adult magnetic amusement products that do not meet the listed criteria likely pose that danger. The criteria it lists include a magnetic flux index of less than 0.5 T2mm2 (50 kG2mm2), or a magnet that does not fit entirely in the small parts cylinder. It excludes toys for a child under 14, which the Toys Regulations cover.
  • Health Canada: Magnetic toys. The page says swallowing more than one small powerful magnet is especially dangerous because the magnets can attract through the intestines, twist them, and tear the intestinal walls. It says to buy magnetic toys whose pieces are too big to swallow, and to get medical help immediately if a child may have swallowed a magnet.

Questions

Is a kids’ magnetic building set the same thing as a high-powered magnet set?

CPSC says the magnet rule in 16 CFR part 1262 does not apply to toys for children under 14, because 16 CFR part 1250 already covers those toys. Health Canada’s magnet assessment also excludes toys for a child under 14 and points to the Toys Regulations. A novelty set sold for adult entertainment is the product those pages tell you to avoid when the magnets are small and powerful.

What size and strength do the official pages name?

CPSC says a loose or separable magnet that fits in the small parts cylinder must have a flux index less than 50 kG2 mm2 under 16 CFR part 1262. Health Canada’s assessment lists the same flux figure, 50 kG2mm2, or a magnet that does not fit entirely in the small parts cylinder, as criteria for the products in that assessment.

Where do I search a magnet recall?

Search the brand and the set name on CPSC Recalls for a US purchase and on Health Canada recalls and safety alerts for a Canadian purchase. Read the age label before you treat a toy and a novelty set as the same product.
