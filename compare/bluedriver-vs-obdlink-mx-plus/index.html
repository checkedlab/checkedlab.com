How to read this

Bluetooth and warranty lines BlueDriver and OBDLink print for the Pro scanner and the MX+.

Each line below is copied from that model’s maker page. The Source link opens that page. A spec that only one card lists is left out of the table. Recall lines use the 2026-11-12 (UTC) catalogue check.

Both are Bluetooth adapters for the OBD-II port, and the shared rows are Bluetooth and warranty. BlueDriver’s product page says it connects to iOS and Android. OBDLink’s MX+ page says Class 2 Bluetooth v3.0 and lists iOS, Android, and Windows.

The BlueDriver terms list a 1-year limited warranty. The MX+ page says a 3 year warranty and a 180-day money-back guarantee. Voltage, size, and the SW-CAN line are on the MX+ card only.

Two plugs, two different spec blocks

BlueDriver’s product page describes a Bluetooth scan tool for 1996 and newer cars, SUVs, and light trucks sold in North America, including most gas, diesel, and hybrid vehicles, and says it supports all major OBD2 protocols. The MX+ page says the adapter works with all 1996 and newer cars and light trucks sold in the United States, supports all legislated OBD-II protocols, and adds SW-CAN and MS-CAN for Ford and GM networks. Those coverage sentences are not the same label, so they stay in the writeup.

The MX+ specification table also prints 12V systems with an 8 to 18 V operating range, dimensions of 1.97 x 1.77 x 0.91 in, and a device weight of 3 oz. None of those labels are on the BlueDriver card. BlueDriver’s card still carries the older lines for a North American origin and a Bluetooth connection to the OBD port. The Bluetooth row in this table uses the current product-page sentence.

Pick the app list or the protocol line

Pick the BlueDriver if the phone list you are matching is iOS and Android, and the warranty sentence you are matching is the 1-year limited warranty on the BlueDriver terms. The product page also names enhanced diagnostics, with makes such as GM, Ford, Chrysler, Toyota, Honda, and others printed under that heading. A make on that list is a BlueDriver sentence, not a promise that every module on every car is covered. The page says enhanced diagnostics may not yet be offered on your vehicle.

Pick the MX+ if the Bluetooth line you are matching is Class 2 Bluetooth v3.0, the app list includes Windows as well as iOS and Android, or you need the SW-CAN and MS-CAN sentence. The warranty on that page is 3 years, plus the 180-day money-back guarantee. The Innova 5210 card on this site is a different scanner, with its own engine and ABS line. It is linked here because people cross-shop the three names.

A Bluetooth version number and a sentence that only says Bluetooth are both connection facts, and they are not the same fact. Read the string on the page you are about to order.
