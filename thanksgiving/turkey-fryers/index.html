Turkey fryers

What CPSC, Health Canada, and NFPA say about outdoor oil turkey fryers, indoor oil fryers, and air fryers, and which notices name a model or item number.

Turkey fryersPressure cookersTurkey temperaturesKitchen fires

What to read on the label

  • On an outdoor oil turkey fryer, read the item or model number on the leg or the rating label, and read whether the unit is propane. The November 26, 2003 CPSC turkey-fryer release says to keep the fryer in full view while the burner is on, to place it in an open area away from walls, fences, and other structures, and never to use it in, on, or under a garage, breezeway, carport, porch, or any structure that can catch fire. It says to leave at least 2 feet between the liquid-propane tank and the burner, to center the pot, and to thaw and dry the turkey. It says partially frozen or wet poultry can splatter hot oil. It says that if a fire occurs, call 911 and do not try to extinguish it with water.
  • The NFPA Educational Messages Desk Reference says turkey fryers that use cooking oil, as currently designed, are not suitable for safe use by even a well-informed and careful consumer, because they use a substantial quantity of oil at high temperatures and the burners can ignite spilled oil. It says NFPA urges people who want fried turkey to buy it from a grocery store, specialty food retailer, or restaurant, or to use an alternative such as an infrared fryer, an air fryer, or an electric turkey fryer that is listed by a nationally recognized testing laboratory. It also says not to leave fryers unattended.
  • On an air fryer or an indoor oil fryer, read the model number on the bottom label or the back of the unit, and read it again in the manual. An air fryer is a different product from an oil turkey fryer. Match the number on the unit to the number on the notice.

How to search a recall

For a purchase in the United States, search the brand and the model on CPSC Recalls. For a purchase in Canada, search the same words on Health Canada recalls and safety alerts. Open the notice and match the model number on the unit. A family name on the box can point at a different model.

The November 19, 2025 CPSC holiday cooking release uses the word COOK for a turkey fryer: carefully follow the manufacturer’s instructions, never overheat the oil and use an oil with a high smoke point, never overfill the oil, and keep the fryer out of the garage, off the deck, and away from the house.

A propane fish fryer is not a turkey fryer. The Health Canada notice below names one fish-fryer model. Read that model number before treating another propane fryer as the same product.

Patterns in official notices

Each pattern below is one official page. The sentence is what that page says.

  • CPSC: Academy Sports + Outdoors recalls the Outdoor Gourmet Turkey Keg. The notice, recall number 19-067, is dated February 19, 2019 and covers about 3,000 Outdoor Gourmet Turkey Keg turkey fryers. It says the fryer is stainless steel, powered by propane, and measures 34 inches high by 17 inches wide including the legs. It says item number 157826 is on a label on one of the four legs. It says the spout can leak oil, posing a fire hazard. It says the firm had two reports of leaking and no reported injuries or property damage. It tells owners to stop using the fryer and return it to an Academy Sports + Outdoors store.
  • CPSC: Safety tips for turkey fryers, November 26, 2003. The release says that since 1998 CPSC had reports of 75 turkey-fryer incidents involving fires, flames, or burns, and that 28 of those were reported for 2002. It says most reported incidents happened while the oil was heating, before the turkey was added, and that any smoke from the oil means the oil is overheated and the burner should be turned off. It says to raise and lower food slowly, to cover bare skin, and to check the oil temperature often. It says that if oil begins to smoke, turn the gas supply off immediately.
  • NFPA: Educational Messages Desk Reference: turkey fryers. Section 8.12 says to use a turkey fryer listed by a nationally recognized testing laboratory, such as an infrared fryer, an air fryer, or an electric turkey fryer, and not to leave fryers unattended. It says oil turkey fryers use a substantial quantity of cooking oil at high temperatures, that home units pose a significant danger that hot oil will be released during cooking, and that the burners can ignite spilled oil. It says that use can lead to devastating burns, other injuries, and destruction of property.
  • CPSC: NESCO stainless steel deep fryers recalled. The notice is dated November 6, 2003 and covers about 47,000 NESCO stainless steel deep fryers with a black plastic top and handles on either side. It names model numbers DF-250T, DF-1250T, and DF-1250TL, printed on the back. It says the mesh filter in the cover can let moisture build in the oil reservoir, so the reservoir can boil over during or shortly after use, posing a burn hazard. It says there were eight reports, including one report of minor burns that needed medical attention. It tells owners to stop using the fryer and contact The Metal Ware Corp. for a replacement cover kit.
  • CPSC: Cosori air fryers recalled for a wire connection that can overheat. The recall alert, number 23-130, is dated February 23, 2023. It says a wire connection can overheat, posing fire and burn hazards. It says the recall involves listed Cosori model numbers, that Cosori is on the front, and that the model number is on the bottom label and in the user manual. It says the units are 3.7 and 5.8 quart. It says about 2 million were sold in the United States and about 250,000 in Canada. It says the firm had received 205 reports of the air fryers catching fire, burning, melting, overheating, or smoking, including 10 reports of minor superficial burn injuries and 23 reports of minor property damage. It tells owners to stop using a recalled unit and contact Cosori.
  • Health Canada: Certain Cosori air fryers recalled due to a potential fire hazard. The notice says the wire connectors can overheat, posing a potential burn and fire hazard. It says the model number is on the bottom label and in the user manual, and the batch number is on the bottom or at the back below the rear exhaust vent. It says that as of February 2023 the company had 205 reports globally, including 56 in Canada, of the air fryers catching fire, burning, melting, overheating, or smoking, and 10 reports of minor superficial burn injuries, including 4 in Canada. It tells owners to stop using the recalled product and contact Cosori for a replacement. The model list on this notice is the one to use for a Canadian purchase.
  • Health Canada: Valley Sportsman propane fish fryer recalled. The notice, identification number RA-68596, is dated December 18, 2018. It names the Valley Sportsman 9.5 litre propane aluminum fish fryer, model 2A-CM186, UPC 8680571. It says a small hole near the handle could let hot cooking oil drip from the pan, posing a burn hazard. It says about 2,468 units were sold in Canada from December 2016 to October 2018, and that as of December 12, 2018 the company had no report of incidents or injuries in Canada. It tells owners to stop using it and return it to Princess Auto. This notice does not name a turkey fryer.

Questions

Where do I search a turkey fryer or an air fryer?

Search the brand and the item or model number on CPSC Recalls for a US purchase and on Health Canada recalls and safety alerts for a Canadian purchase. The Academy notice names item 157826. The Cosori notices say the model number is on the bottom label. The Valley Sportsman notice names fish-fryer model 2A-CM186.

What does NFPA say about an oil turkey fryer?

The NFPA Educational Messages Desk Reference says oil turkey fryers, as currently designed, are not suitable for safe use by even a well-informed and careful consumer. It names an infrared fryer, an air fryer, or an electric turkey fryer listed by a nationally recognized testing laboratory as alternatives, and it says not to leave fryers unattended. An air fryer still has its own model-number check.

Is the COSORI Pro II card one of the recalled Cosori models?

The catalogue page says the Pro II card’s model line reads CS358-AF series and that CS358-AF is not among the model numbers copied from the February 23, 2023 notices. Read the bottom label and compare it with the Cosori 2023 model list.
