KitchenAid Artisan vs Classic: which to buy

The Artisan is the 5 quart tilt-head mixer. KitchenAid’s 24 October 2025 comparison puts that motor at 325 watts and the Classic motor at 275 watts. The Classic is the 4.5 quart mixer, in white and onyx black, with a brushed bowl and no handle. Both have 10 speeds, the same listed cabinet size, and a one-year limited warranty on that comparison.

Choose the Artisan if

  • You want the 5 quart bowl with a handle.
  • You want the 325 watt motor and more than 20 colors.
  • You want the 9-dozen cookie line, using the flat beater and 28g of dough each.

How to read this

The table keeps a row only when both makers print that label. The Source link opens the sentence the number came from. A figure only one maker prints sits under this fold. The recall line is the CPSC and Health Canada check dated 2026-11-13 (UTC).

KitchenAid’s Artisan product page is the 5 quart tilt-head, model KSM150PS, with 10 speeds. The Classic product page is the 4.5 quart tilt-head. The 24 October 2025 KitchenAid article names that Classic as K45SS and puts the motors at 325 watts and 275 watts.

The article says both cabinets are 14 1/10 by 8 7/10 by 13 9/10 inches, and both have a one-year limited warranty. Cookie capacity on the product pages is up to 9 dozen for the Artisan and up to 8 dozen for the Classic, each using the flat beater and 28g of dough.

KitchenAid Artisan vs Classic bowl, motor, and speeds

Both product pages describe a tilt-head stand mixer with 10 speeds, from stir up to whipping cream at speed 8, and both say the metal body has 59 touchpoints around the bowl. The Artisan page says the bowl is 5 quart stainless steel with a comfortable handle, and that it is dishwasher-safe. The Classic page says the bowl is 4.5 quart stainless steel and dishwasher-safe. The October 2025 article is more specific on finish and handle: the Artisan bowl is polished stainless with a handle, and the Classic bowl is brushed stainless without a handle.

Watts are on the article, not in the short feature blocks of the two product pages opened here. The article says both motors are alternating current, the Artisan is 325 watts, and the Classic is 275 watts, and that the extra power can help with thick, heavy doughs. Speeds are not the split. Ten speeds is the line on both product pages and on the article.

What is in the box, and the pouring-shield lines

The Artisan Empire Red page says model KSM150PS includes five accessories: the mixer, a 5 quart stainless bowl, a coated flat beater, a coated dough hook, and a 6-wire whip. The Classic onyx black page says the included set is the mixer, a bowl that fits model K45, a coated dough hook, a coated flat beater, and a 6-wire whip. Hub tools beyond that are optional on both pages. Each page says there are more than 10 attachments, sold separately, and the article says hub attachments work on any KitchenAid stand mixer, with a note that the ice cream maker attachment is not for the Artisan Mini.

The pouring shield is where the official lines disagree. The 24 October 2025 article says the 5 quart Artisan tilt-head mixers come with a pouring shield, and that the Secure Fit pouring shield can be bought separately for 4.5 and 5 quart tilt-head mixers with stainless bowls. The Empire Red include list opened here does not name a pouring shield. Read the include list on the color you are ordering before you assume the shield is in that box.

The article also says the comparison is the standard Artisan KSM150PS against the Classic K45SS, not the Artisan Mini, not a color-of-the-year bundle, and not the Deluxe. The Deluxe, on that same article, is a 4.5 quart mixer with a 300 watt motor and a handled polished bowl. It is a third machine.

Colors, model codes, and what to confirm

The Artisan product page says the mixer comes in over 20 colors, and the color list on that page runs well past twenty names. The Classic page shows two: White and Onyx Black. The article says the same thing in different words: more than 20 colors for the Artisan, and two neutral shades for the Classic. The article also says the cabinet size is the same, so color and bowl are the reasons to move between them, not a smaller footprint.

Model codes need a careful read. The Artisan card on this site uses KSM150PSER, which is the Empire Red address of the KSM150PS series the product page names. The Classic article names K45SS. The onyx black address is K45SSOB, and the include heading on that onyx black page says Model K45SSW. Match K45SS, then match the color code on the box you are buying.

Pick the Artisan if you want 5 quarts, 325 watts, a handled bowl, and the wide color list. Pick the Classic if you want 4.5 quarts, 275 watts, and white or black. Both warranty lines on the article are one year limited. The Black Friday note for the Artisan is a separate write-up of what to confirm before you pay. It does not change these mixer figures.

Questions

What is the bowl difference between Artisan and Classic?

The Artisan product page says a 5 quart bowl. The Classic product page says a 4.5 quart bowl. KitchenAid’s 24 October 2025 article says the Artisan bowl is polished stainless with a handle and the Classic bowl is brushed stainless without a handle.

How many watts does KitchenAid list?

The same article says 325 watts for the Artisan KSM150PS and 275 watts for the Classic K45SS. Both are described as alternating-current motors. The product-page feature blocks opened here do not print the watt number.

Are the mixers the same size?

The article says both are 14 1/10 by 8 7/10 by 13 9/10 inches, and that they fit under standard upper cabinets.

Do both come with a pouring shield?

The article says the Artisan comes with a pouring shield and that a shield for the Classic is sold separately. The Empire Red include list names the mixer, bowl, flat beater, dough hook, and 6-wire whip, and does not name a shield. Read the include list for the color you order.

How many colors and what warranty?

The Artisan page says over 20 colors. The Classic page shows white and onyx black. The article says both have a one-year limited warranty from the date of purchase.
