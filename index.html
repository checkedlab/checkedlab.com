How to use this site

Checked Lab is a list of named models for shoppers in the United States and Canada. A card names one model, copies specs from that model's product page, and records a dated recall check. The US match uses the CPSC SaferProducts feed.

The Canadian match uses Health Canada's open recall dataset. This page is the way in to eight category hubs, the spec comparisons, the gift guides, and the Black Friday page. How we check says what the date on a card means.

Start with the model number, not the family name. Foodi, Cosori, Shark, Dyson, Levoit, and NOCO each have more than one card here. The string on the rating plate, the box, and the listing should match the card.

If the listing uses the brand and a different number, the recall line on this card is about a different product. The explainers for the Cosori 2023 air fryer notices, the Ninja Foodi OP300 notices, and the Lasko heater notices quote the official model lists and point at the model card that is actually named. A familiar photo is not a model number.

Read the spec in the unit the maker printed. The Coway Airmega Mighty card states a smoke CADR and a room size at 4.8 air changes per hour, from the Coway page. The Levoit Core 600S card states its room size twice, once at one air change per hour and once at 4.8, from the Levoit page.

Those are different sentences. Robot vacuums on the cleaning hub state suction in pascals. The Dyson cordless cards state suction in air watts.

Pascals and air watts are different units, so a comparison keeps only labels both product pages share. Weight, run time, tank size, and an engine-size limit are useful because the product page attaches a number and a unit.

Ignore a marketing line that the product page for that model number does not print. A room-size claim that uses one air change per hour will look much larger than the same machine's figure at about five air changes. A peak current on a jump starter is the peak the maker printed, not a second number this site invents.

A filter certification matters when the maker names the standard, as the Aquasana Claryum card does with NSF/ANSI 42, 53 including P473, and 401. If the card does not name a standard, do not borrow one from a different model.

The recall line is the part to read before you pay. Search the same model on CPSC Recalls for a US purchase and on Health Canada recalls and safety alerts for a Canadian purchase. Recall checked means the brand and model were matched and no record matched that model on the printed date.

Official recall found means the brand and the model token both appear in one recall record, and the notice link is on the card. A clear line for one model number does not cover the next number in the same brand. Prices stay on the store.

The buttons open that store's page or its search for the brand and model.

From here, kitchen covers cookware and appliances, cleaning covers robots and sticks, and air and water covers purifiers, filters, humidifiers, and dehumidifiers. Winter covers heat, humidity, and snow tools, and the winter safety guides add the label lines for heaters, heated bedding, alarms, and outage power. Car and garage, pet, sleep, and personal tech are the other hubs. Gift guides regroup the same cards by recipient. Black Friday repeats the model check for that shopping window.

Questions

What does a card on Checked Lab actually show?

One model, specs copied from that model's product page, and a dated recall check. The US match uses the CPSC SaferProducts feed. The Canadian match uses Health Canada's open recall dataset.

How we check is the longer note on what the date means.

What should I match before I trust a recall line?

The model number on the unit, the box, and the listing. A family name such as Foodi, Cosori, Shark, or Dyson covers more than one card. A clear line on one model number does not cover the next number in the same brand.

Why do two room-size numbers on one card disagree?

They are often different air-change rates. The Levoit Core 600S page lists 2,909 sq ft at 1 air change per hour and 606 sq ft at 4.8. Read both sentences.

Do not keep only the larger one.

Where is the price?

On the store. Buttons open that store's page or a search for the brand and model. This site does not print a price.

How do I check a model that is not on a card?

Search the model on CPSC Recalls for a US purchase and on Health Canada recalls and safety alerts for a Canadian purchase. Read the model numbers printed on any notice. The recall checks page covers only the models listed here.

How we check

  1. Match the model. The brand and model are matched to the CPSC SaferProducts feed and the Health Canada open dataset. The date on the card is the day that match was recorded.
  2. Copy the published specs. The numbers come from the brand’s product page, with the source link beside them.
  3. Open the store. The buttons search that store for the brand and model. Prices stay on the store.
