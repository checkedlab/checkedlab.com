All-Clad D3 vs D5: which to buy

Both are 12-inch stainless fry pans that All-Clad rates for the oven and broiler up to 600 F, on any stovetop including induction, with a limited lifetime warranty and hand washing. The D3, model 4112, is 3-ply. All-Clad describes fast heat-up and even heating from tri-ply bonding to the rim. The D5, model SD55112, is 5-ply. All-Clad calls it the premier range, at a higher price, with heat that spreads more evenly and slowly.

Choose the D3 if

  • You want the 3-ply fry pan, model 4112.
  • You want fast heat-up and even heating from tri-ply bonding to the rim.
  • You want the lower price All-Clad lists for this 12-inch pan.

How to read this

A row is shown when both makers print that label. Source opens the sentence the figure came from. Details only one of them prints are listed under that name below.

Check the model number on CPSC (US) and Health Canada (CA) recalls before buying.

All-Clad’s D3 12-inch fry pan is model 4112. The D5 12-inch polished fry pan is model SD55112. Both pages say stainless steel, a polished finish, oven and broiler safe up to 600 F, any stovetop including induction, hand washing, and a limited lifetime warranty.

Construction is the split. The D3 page says tri-ply construction that combines five metal layers into three functional layers, for fast heat-up and even heating. The D5 page says 5-ply bonded construction, and calls D5 the premier range for supremely even heating. All-Clad lists the D5 12-inch fry pan at a higher price than the D3.

All-Clad D3 vs D5 is a ply difference on the same 12-inch size

Both fry pans are titled 12 inch, and both spec tables say Size 12 inch, Material Stainless Steel, Exterior Finish Polished Stainless Steel, Made in United States, and Piece Count 1. Both detail blocks say the cookware is made in the USA with global components, bonded, engineered, and assembled in Canonsburg, Pennsylvania. Both say the handles are stainless and double-riveted, and both mention flared edges for pouring.

The D3 description says fully bonded tri-ply construction combining five metal layers into three functional layers: two stainless layers bonded to a three-layer aluminum and aluminum-alloy core. Read that sentence twice. It says three functional layers, and it also says five metal layers inside those three. The D5 description says 5-ply bonded construction, with alternating layers of aluminum and stainless steel, and calls D5 the premier range for even heating. A shopper who hears “five” on both pages is hearing two different sentences.

Oven and stovetop lines match. Both say oven and broiler safe up to 600 F, and both say extended heat over 500 F can change the color of the stainless without affecting performance. Both say high heat is for boiling, and that everyday cooking should start on low to moderate heat. Both say the pan works on any stovetop, including induction. Both say handwash only. The care blocks match closely: wash before first use, cool the pan before washing, and do not use oven cleaner, steel wool, or bleach.

Heat, the premier range, and two lines to ignore

All-Clad’s D3 cooking notes say tri-ply bonding all the way to the rim gives fast heat-up and even heating. The aluminum in the core is there for heat conductivity, and the stainless layers are there for durability. The D5 notes go further. Patented five-ply bonding to the rim is described as supremely even heating, with fewer hot spots. A question on the D5 page says the stainless and aluminum layers distribute heat more evenly and slowly. All-Clad calls D5 its premier range for that even heating, thanks to the extra layers.

Price follows the same split. On the day these pages were opened, the D3 12-inch fry pan was the lower price and the D5 12-inch fry pan was the higher price. All-Clad can change either number. The tier is the one on the two product pages, with D5 named as the premier range. Care, oven temperature, and induction are not a reason to pick one. Both say oven and broiler safe up to 600 F, any stovetop including induction, and hand washing.

Two printed fields are poor reasons to choose. The D3 spec table prints Dimensions 20.1 x 12.5 x 4.4 inches. The D5 spec table prints Dimensions 15.0 x 8.6 x 2.9 inches on a pan titled 12 inch. Those lines do not describe the same footprint. The weight fields print 3.000000 on the D3 and 4.000000 on the D5, and neither line names pounds or kilograms. Use the ply, the heat sentences, and the price instead. Both pages were in stock when opened. That can change. Match model 4112 or SD55112 on the box. UPC on the D3 page is 011644502201. UPC on the D5 page is 011644010089.

How to choose, then what to confirm

Pick the D3 if you want model 4112, the 3-ply construction, and the fast heat-up All-Clad describes, at the lower of the two prices. Pick the D5 if you want model SD55112, the 5-ply construction, the slower even heat, and the premier range.

Care is not a reason to pick one. Both tell you to preheat on low to moderate heat, dry food before it goes in, and let stainless release before you turn it. Both warn that salt in a cold pan can pit the inside, and that a hot pan should not go under cold water. The limited lifetime warranty covers All-Clad cookware on both pages. Read that warranty text on the pan you order. The D3 model page is the 12-inch fry pan already listed here, not a saucepan and not an 8-inch skillet.

Questions

Is All-Clad D5 the same metal stack as D3?

No. The D3 page says tri-ply construction that combines five metal layers into three functional layers. The D5 page says 5-ply bonded construction.

Are both 12-inch pans oven safe to the same temperature?

Yes. Both pages say oven and broiler safe up to 600 F, and both say heat over 500 F can discolor stainless without changing performance.

Can both go on induction?

Yes. Both pages say the pan is compatible with any stovetop, including induction.

Why do the dimension lines look different?

The D3 12-inch page prints 20.1 x 12.5 x 4.4 inches. The D5 12-inch page prints 15.0 x 8.6 x 2.9 inches. A 12-inch title with an 8.6-inch dimension line needs a tape measure. Those lines are not a reason to pick one pan.

What models and warranties are these?

D3 is model 4112. D5 is model SD55112. Both pages say a limited lifetime warranty, hand washing, and a polished stainless finish. All-Clad calls D5 its premier range and lists it at the higher price.
