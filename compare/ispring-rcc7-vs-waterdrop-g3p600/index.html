How to read this

The RCC7 is a 5-stage system at 75 gallons a day, and the G3P600 is an 8-stage system at 600 gallons a day.

Each line below is copied from that model’s maker page. The Source link opens that page. A spec that only one card lists is left out of the table. Recall lines use the 2026-11-12 (UTC) catalogue check.

The iSpring card calls the daily figure Output and prints 75 gallons per day. The Waterdrop card calls its daily figure Capacity and prints 600 gallons per day. This table uses Capacity for both so the gallons sit on one row. Stages are 5 on the iSpring page and 8 on the Waterdrop page.

A stage count is how the maker numbers the system. It is not a list of the same contaminants. Read the certification sentence on the page you are buying from.

Tank system and tankless system

These two cards are both under-sink reverse osmosis, and the shared rows are stages, daily gallons, and certifications. iSpring prints 5 stages, 75 gallons per day, and NSF/ANSI 58. Waterdrop prints 8 stages, 600 gallons per day, and NSF/ANSI 42, 58, and 372. The gallons are not close. A reader who stops at the word reverse osmosis has not read the capacity line.

The Waterdrop card also states a 2:1 pure-to-drain ratio. That label is not on the iSpring card, so the ratio stays off the table. The iSpring card states a membrane down to 0.0001 microns. That label is not on the Waterdrop card. The iSpring page is the one that names a storage-tank style of system in this catalogue. The Waterdrop G3P600 card does not print a tank volume.

Pick the gallons line you can cite

Pick the RCC7 if the daily figure you are matching is 75 gallons, the stage count is 5, or you need the NSF/ANSI 58 line and the 0.0001 micron membrane line. Those last two are the iSpring wording. The card labels the gallons Output. The table repeats them under Capacity so they sit beside Waterdrop’s gallons.

Pick the G3P600 if the daily figure you are matching is 600 gallons, the stage count is 8, or you need the 2:1 pure-to-drain line and the certification line that names NSF/ANSI 42, 58, and 372. A higher stated stage count is not, by itself, a longer contaminant list. Open the certification sentence.

The Brita and ZeroWater comparison on this site is a pitcher pair, not an under-sink pair. The G3P600 and APEC comparison is the other under-sink pair, and it uses the same Waterdrop card.
