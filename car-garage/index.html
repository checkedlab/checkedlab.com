How to choose car and garage gear

Car and garage cards cover jump starters, battery chargers, compressors, scan tools, and a handful of shop tools. They do not share one rating. A jump starter is limited by the peak current and the engine sizes the maker prints.

A charger is limited by voltage, amps, and the battery types the page names. A compressor is limited by flow, maximum pressure, and duty. A meter is limited by the category rating printed on it.

Match the model number first. GB40 and GB50 are both NOCO, and they are not the same card.

Read peak current as peak current. The NOCO GB40 page lists 1,000 A at 12 V, gas engines up to 6 L, diesel engines up to 3 L, 2.4 lb, and IP65. The GB50 page lists 1,500 A, gas up to 7 L, diesel up to 4.5 L, 2.7 lb, and IP65. GB40 and GB50 keeps those shared labels.

If the engine size is inside the smaller limit, both pages name it. If it sits between the two limits, only the GB50 line names that size. This site does not print a continuous amp figure the NOCO page left out.

IP65 is the enclosure line both pages state. It is not a claim about cranking time.

Chargers and inflators need the same honesty about two numbers. The Battery Tender Junior page lists 12 V at 750 mA and batteries from 3 Ah to 30 Ah. The CTEK MXS 5.0 page lists 12 V, 1.2 to 110 Ah, and maintenance charging to 160 Ah.

Those ranges are not the same charger. The Sun Joe SPX3000 manual states 2030 PSI maximum and 1450 PSI rated, with 1.76 GPM maximum flow. Keep both pressure lines.

The VIAIR 88P page lists 1.97 CFM at 0 PSI, 120 PSI maximum, and a 25 minute duty at 30 PSI. The Fanttik X8 Apex page lists up to 150 PSI. A maximum and a rated figure are both real, and they are not synonyms.

Shop tools are limited by the range and the safety category the maker prints. The Fluke 117 page lists CAT III 600 V. The TEKTON 24335 page lists a 1/2 inch drive, 10 to 150 ft-lb, and accuracy of plus or minus 4 percent.

The DeWalt DCD771 page lists two speed ranges, 0-450 and 1,500 rpm, and a 1/2 in chuck. Scan tools differ by which modules they name. The Innova 5210 page lists engine and ABS.

The OBDLink MX+ page lists the protocols it adds. Read the module list on the page you are buying. A 'reads your car' line is not a module list.

Ignore a peak-amp slogan that is higher than the model page. Ignore a single PSI number when the manual prints both a maximum and a rated pressure. The recall check is the model match, including when a notice names a different product in the same brand.

US cards use the CPSC SaferProducts feed. Canadian cards use Health Canada's open dataset. Search CPSC Recalls and Health Canada recalls and safety alerts.

A clear line on the GB40 does not cover the GB50. How we check says what the date means. Gift guides for the garage regroup some of these cards by recipient.

Questions

What does peak current mean on the NOCO cards?

It is the peak the product page prints. GB40 states 1,000 A at 12 V. GB50 states 1,500 A.

The pages also state gas and diesel engine limits. This site does not add a continuous figure the page left out.

Why does the pressure washer list two PSI numbers?

Because the Sun Joe manual states both. Maximum pressure is 2030 PSI. Rated pressure is 1450 PSI.

Keep both. VIAIR states a maximum of 120 PSI and a duty of 25 minutes at 30 PSI, which is a third kind of limit.

How do I tell the two 12 V chargers apart?

By the battery range on the page. Battery Tender Junior states 3 Ah to 30 Ah at 750 mA. CTEK MXS 5.0 states 1.2 to 110 Ah, with maintenance charging to 160 Ah.

The amp line and the amp-hour line are doing different work.

How does the recall check work for a garage tool?

The US line matches the brand and model to the CPSC SaferProducts feed. The Canadian line uses Health Canada's open dataset. A notice for another product in the same brand can still exist, and the model page links it when the list has that record.

A clear GB40 line does not cover the GB50.

What should I read on a multimeter card?

The safety category the maker prints. The Fluke 117 page lists CAT III 600 V, a 6,000 count display, and a 9 V battery. That category line is the one to match to the work, not a slogan about electricians.
