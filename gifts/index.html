How to choose a gift from this catalogue

A gift page is only useful if the person can use the object and you can still name the model. These guides regroup catalogue cards by recipient. They do not invent a second set of products.

A guide is published when the catalogue can fill it with at least five models and the recorded US and Canadian checks found no matching recall for those models. The spec line on each pick is copied from the maker fields on that card. How we check says what the date means. The badge on the US view is the CPSC match.

The badge on the CA view is the Health Canada match.

Choose the recipient before you choose the object. A home cook and a coffee drinker can both live in the kitchen and still want different cards. The home-cook guide keeps blenders, stand mixers, multi-cookers, air fryers, Dutch ovens, skillets, and rice cookers.

The coffee guide keeps cards whose text names coffee, espresso, a grinder, a kettle, or pour-over. The Vitamix E310 page states a 2 HP motor and a 48 oz container. The Breville Barista Express page states an integrated conical burr grinder with 16 settings and a 54 mm portafilter.

Those are different gifts even though both sit on a counter.

Read the constraint that actually limits the gift. Weight and footprint matter in a small place. The Levoit Core 300S-P page states 8.7 × 8.7 × 14.2 in and 7.48 lb.

The Dyson V8 page states 5.58 lb. A pet gift should match the animal the maker sized. The FURminator medium short-hair tool page states a medium dog, 25 to 50 lb, short hair.

The Litter-Robot 4 page states a 22 × 27 in footprint and a household of up to 4 cats. Do not treat a pet-named vacuum as a substitute for a tool whose page states a coat type.

Ignore three things that show up on gift roundups and do not show up here. Ignore a ranked list. Ignore a rating line.

Ignore a price printed next to the model, because prices stay on the store and the store button is on the card. Also ignore a family name used as if it were one product. If the gift is an air fryer, open the Crispi card, the Cosori Pro II card, and the Cosori 2023 explainer before you buy.

The explainer quotes the notice. The cards quote the maker pages. Black Friday is the same check aimed at that shopping window.

Two lists on this hub are not catalogue grids. Gifts for kids names mainstream kids products and the official check to do. Gifts for the host does the same for holiday hosting. Toy safety covers button and coin batteries, magnets, water beads, and ride-ons. Thanksgiving covers turkey fryers, pressure cookers, turkey temperatures, and kitchen fires. Use those pages when the gift is for a child or for a holiday table. Use the catalogue guides when the model is already a card.

The recall step does not change because the occasion is a gift. Search the model on CPSC Recalls for a US purchase and on Health Canada recalls and safety alerts for a Canadian purchase. The catalogue match uses the CPSC SaferProducts feed and Health Canada's open dataset.

A clear line on the model you wrap does not cover a different model number in the same brand. Recall checks is the full list.

Questions

What does the recall line on a gift pick mean?

It is the recorded check for that model. On the US view the source is CPSC. On the CA view the source is Health Canada. The date is the day that check was recorded. No matching recall was found for that model as of that date. A notice for a different model can still exist, and the model page links it.

Where do the spec lines come from?

Each spec line is copied from the maker fields stored for that model. The model page links the maker source.

Which recipient lists are published?

The lists are the recipient groups this catalogue can fill with at least five models: Home cook, Coffee lovers, The home, Pet owners, Tech and gadgets, The garage, and Sleep.
