How to read this

Stages and capacity as the Waterdrop and APEC pages state them for the G3P600 and the ROES-50, with a dated recall line for each.

Each line below is copied from that model’s maker page. The Source link opens that page. A spec that only one card lists is left out of the table. Recall lines use the 2026-11-12 (UTC) catalogue check.

The Waterdrop page states 8 stages and a capacity of 600 gallons per day. The APEC page states 5 stages and a capacity of 50 gallons per day at 60 psi.

A higher stated stage count and a higher stated daily capacity are the Waterdrop lines. Ratio and certifications are on the Waterdrop card only. Tank and dimensions are on the APEC card only.

Both pages name stages and a daily capacity

The shared labels are stages and capacity. Waterdrop prints 8 stages and 600 gallons per day. APEC prints 5 stages and 50 gallons per day at 60 psi. The APEC capacity line includes a pressure, 60 psi. The Waterdrop capacity line does not print a pressure in that field. The numbers are both “gallons per day,” and the APEC line is the one that names the test pressure.

Waterdrop also states a 2:1 pure-to-drain ratio and certifications of NSF/ANSI 42, 58, and 372. APEC also states a 4 gallon tank and dimensions of 15.5 in L x 5.25 in W x 17 in H. Those labels are not shared, so the table leaves them on the cards.

Pick by daily gallons, tank, or the certification line

Pick the Waterdrop page if the capacity figure you are matching is 600 gallons per day, the stage count you are matching is 8, or you need the 2:1 pure-to-drain line and the NSF/ANSI 42, 58, and 372 line. Those last two are Waterdrop-only labels.

Pick the APEC page if the capacity figure you are matching is 50 gallons per day at 60 psi, the stage count is 5, or you need the 4 gallon tank line and the 15.5 x 5.25 x 17 in dimension line. A tankless reading of the Waterdrop card is not a tank spec. The APEC page is the one that states a tank volume.

A stage count is not a list of contaminants. The pages state how many stages they count. They do not, in these fields, name the same contaminant table. Read the certification line where a page prints one. The recall block is the catalogue match for each system.
