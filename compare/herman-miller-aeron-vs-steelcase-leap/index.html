How to read this

Seat height, chair weight, weight limit, and warranty as Herman Miller and Steelcase print them for Aeron and Leap.

Each line below is copied from that model’s maker page. The Source link opens that page. A spec that only one card lists is left out of the table. Recall lines use the 2026-11-12 (UTC) catalogue check.

Aeron is three sizes on the Herman Miller store page. Leap’s catalogue card is one work chair: seat height 15.5 to 20.5 in, chair weight 46.7 lb, a 400 lb limit, and a 12-year limited warranty on parts and labor.

The Aeron store page prints a 12-year warranty, size A up to 300 lb, size B up to 350 lb, and size C up to 400 lb. A single 400 lb line and a three-size limit line are not the same claim.

One size line against three

The shared labels are seat height, chair weight, limit, and warranty. On the Aeron store page, size A lists a seat height from 14¾ to 19 in and a chair weight of 40 lb. Size B lists 16 to 20½ in and 41 lb. Size C lists 16 to 20½ in and 43 lb. The same page’s general product-weight line says 41 lb, which matches the size B figure. Leap’s card states 15.5 to 20.5 in and 46.7 lb. A lower stated chair weight, at the size you actually order, is an Aeron line. Leap’s 46.7 lb is the single figure on that card.

Limits differ in shape. Aeron’s store page caps size A at 300 lb, size B at 350 lb, and size C at 400 lb. The Steelcase Leap page says the chair is durable enough to handle weight up to 400 lb, and the same page points Leap Plus at users up to 500 lb. This comparison uses the standard Leap card, not Leap Plus. Do not read Aeron’s size C cap and Leap’s 400 lb line as one shared number until you have picked an Aeron size.

What each maker names, besides the table

The Aeron overview page names three sizes, A, B, and C, an 8Z Pellicle suspension, adjustable PostureFit SL, and Harmonic 2 tilt. Those labels are not on the Leap card, so they stay off the table. The Leap page names LiveBack, a height-adjustable lumbar support, a lower-back firmness control, and arms that adjust in height, width, depth, and pivot. Those labels are not on the Aeron card. A lumbar sentence from one maker is not a medical claim, and it is not the other maker’s sentence.

Both warranty lines say 12 years, and then they diverge. Herman Miller’s store page says 12-year warranty and that terms apply. Steelcase’s Leap page says a limited lifetime warranty of 12 years, multi-shift, 24/7, parts and labor. The catalogue card shortens the Leap line to 12 years limited on parts and labor. Read the full sentence on the Steelcase page before you treat the two 12-year lines as identical.

Pick the size chart or the single chair

Pick the Aeron if you are matching a size. Size A is the 300 lb cap and the 14¾ to 19 in seat. Size B is the 350 lb cap, 16 to 20½ in, and 41 lb. Size C is the 400 lb cap, the same 16 to 20½ in seat line, and 43 lb. The pellicle, PostureFit SL, and Harmonic 2 names are the Aeron overview, not the Leap page.

Pick the Leap if you are matching 46.7 lb, a seat height of 15.5 to 20.5 in, and the up-to-400 lb line on the Leap page, with the 12-year multi-shift warranty sentence. If the person who will sit in it is over that 400 lb line, the Leap page is the one that names Leap Plus at up to 500 lb. That is a different chair from the card on this page.

Seat-height ranges overlap on paper and still come from different measuring notes. Quote the range on the page for the size you order. The gift-guide index links here because both chairs are in the sleep catalogue.
