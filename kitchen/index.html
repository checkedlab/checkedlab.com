How to choose kitchen gear

Kitchen gear on this hub runs from a cast-iron skillet to a 1,800 watt oven. The useful spec is the one that limits the job: capacity, wattage, material, or a dimension that has to fit a cabinet. A family name is not that spec.

Foodi, Ninja, Cosori, and Breville each cover more than one card. Match the model number on the unit to the card. The Cosori 2023 explainer, the Ninja Foodi OP300 explainer, and the Instant Pot notices quote official lists and name which catalogue card is actually in them.

Air fryers and multi-cookers are where listings drift. The Ninja Crispi page states 1,500 W and 4-qt and 6-cup glass containers. The COSORI Pro II page states 5.8 qt and 1,700 W rated power. Crispi and Pro II share dimensions and weight only.

The Ninja Foodi 10-in-1 page states 1,800 W and room for a 12 lb turkey. The Instant Pot Duo Plus page states 6 quarts and 1,000 W. Duo Plus and Foodi is that pair. An oven wattage and a pressure-cooker wattage answer different questions.

Counter tools are limited by the motor, the bowl, or the dose the maker states. The Vitamix E310 page states a 2 HP motor, a 48 oz container, and 10.5 lb. The KitchenAid Artisan page states a 5 quart bowl and 10 speeds.

The Breville Barista Express page states a 54 mm portafilter, an 18 g dose, and 16 grind settings. The Baratza Encore ESP page states 40 mm conical steel burrs and settings split between espresso and filter. The Fellow Stagg EKG page states 1200 W.

Read the setting count the page prints. A 'cafe' word in a title is not a grind setting.

Pans and ovens are limited by material and by the temperature the maker allows. The Lodge 10.25 inch skillet page states 5.66 lb and seasoning with vegetable oil. The Le Creuset 5.5 qt page states enameled cast iron, 5 to 6 servings, and oven use up to 500 F.

The All-Clad D3 12 inch page states oven use up to 600 F. The Breville Smart Oven Air Fryer Pro page states a 1 cubic foot interior. Capacity in quarts, servings, and cubic feet are three different units. Thanksgiving is the separate note for fryers, pressure cookers, turkey temperatures, and kitchen fires.

Ignore a wattage taken from a different model in the same family. Ignore a capacity that mixes a glass container with a sheet-pan oven. The recall check is the model match.

US cards use the CPSC SaferProducts feed. Canadian cards use Health Canada's open dataset. Search CPSC Recalls and Health Canada recalls and safety alerts.

A clear line on the Crispi does not cover the Foodi, and a clear line on one Instant Pot model does not cover another. How we check defines the date on the card. The Vitamix container explainer is the place to read when a notice is about a container rather than the motor.

Questions

Why are there several Ninja and Cosori cards?

Because they are different model numbers. Crispi is FN101, at 1,500 W, with 4-qt and 6-cup glass containers. The Foodi 10-in-1 states 1,800 W.

The Cosori Pro II states 5.8 qt and 1,700 W. A family name on a listing can point at any of them.

What does the comparison leave out, and why?

Any label that only one maker page prints. Crispi and Pro II share dimensions and weight in the table. Power and capacity stay on the cards.

Duo Plus and the Foodi are a separate pair.

How do I read an oven-safe temperature?

As the maximum the maker printed for that piece. Le Creuset states oven use up to 500 F for the 5.5 qt Signature round Dutch oven. All-Clad states oven use up to 600 F for the 12 inch D3 fry pan.

Those limits belong to those model pages.

How does the recall check work for a kitchen model?

The US line matches the brand and model to the CPSC SaferProducts feed. The Canadian line uses Health Canada's open dataset. Open the Cosori, Foodi, Instant Pot, or Vitamix explainer when a notice names a list of models.

A clear line on one model does not cover the next.

Where should I look for holiday cooking hazards?
