All-Clad D3 vs D5: which to buy

Both are 12-inch stainless fry pans that All-Clad rates for the oven and broiler up to 600 F, on any stovetop including induction, with a limited lifetime warranty and hand washing. The D3, model 4112, is the 3-ply pan. The D5, model SD55112, is the 5-ply pan. Choose the D3 for the 3-ply construction. Choose the D5 if you want the extra ply All-Clad calls its premier stainless line.

Choose the D3 if

  • You want the 3-ply 12-inch fry pan, model 4112.
  • You want the spec-table weight that prints as 3, with no unit named.
  • You want the dimension line 20.1 x 12.5 x 4.4 inches.

How to read this

The table keeps a row only when both makers print that label. The Source link opens the sentence the number came from. A figure only one maker prints sits under this fold. The recall line is the CPSC and Health Canada check dated 2026-11-13 (UTC).

All-Clad’s D3 12-inch fry pan is model 4112. The D5 12-inch polished fry pan is model SD55112. Both pages say stainless steel, a polished finish, oven and broiler safe up to 600 F, any stovetop including induction, hand washing, and a limited lifetime warranty.

Construction is the split. The D3 page says tri-ply construction that combines five metal layers into three functional layers. The D5 page says 5-ply bonded construction. The spec tables print Weight 3.000000 for the D3 and 4.000000 for the D5, and neither line names a unit.

All-Clad D3 vs D5 is a ply difference on the same 12-inch size

Both fry pans are titled 12 inch, and both spec tables say Size 12 inch, Material Stainless Steel, Exterior Finish Polished Stainless Steel, Made in United States, and Piece Count 1. Both detail blocks say the cookware is made in the USA with global components, bonded, engineered, and assembled in Canonsburg, Pennsylvania. Both say the handles are stainless and double-riveted, and both mention flared edges for pouring.

The D3 description says fully bonded tri-ply construction combining five metal layers into three functional layers: two stainless layers bonded to a three-layer aluminum and aluminum-alloy core. Read that sentence twice. It says three functional layers, and it also says five metal layers inside those three. The D5 description says 5-ply bonded construction, with alternating layers of aluminum and stainless steel, and calls D5 the premier range for even heating. A shopper who hears “five” on both pages is hearing two different sentences.

Oven and stovetop lines match. Both say oven and broiler safe up to 600 F, and both say extended heat over 500 F can change the color of the stainless without affecting performance. Both say high heat is for boiling, and that everyday cooking should start on low to moderate heat. Both say the pan works on any stovetop, including induction. Both say handwash only. The care blocks match closely: wash before first use, cool the pan before washing, and do not use oven cleaner, steel wool, or bleach.

The dimension lines do not describe the same footprint

The D3 12-inch spec table prints Dimensions 20.1 x 12.5 x 4.4 inches. That is the line on the D3 spec table. The D5 12-inch spec table prints Dimensions 15.0 x 8.6 x 2.9 inches. A pan whose title says 12 inch and whose dimension line says 8.6 inches across is not a line to trust without measuring. This table leaves dimensions off the shared rows for that reason. The D3 figure stays with the D3 notes under the fold.

Weight has a similar problem of a missing unit. The D3 table prints 3.000000. The D5 table prints 4.000000. All-Clad does not write lb, kg, or ounces next to those numbers on the pages opened here. A higher printed number is the D5 line. It is not, by itself, a finished weight you can put on a shelf drawing.

Both pages were in stock when opened. That can change. Match model 4112 or SD55112 on the box, then match 3-ply or 5-ply in the description. UPC on the D3 page is 011644502201. UPC on the D5 page is 011644010089.

How to choose, then what to confirm

Pick the D3 if you want model 4112, the 3-ply sentence, the 20.1 x 12.5 x 4.4 inch dimension line, and the weight number 3.000000. Pick the D5 if you want model SD55112, the 5-ply sentence, and the weight number 4.000000, and you have decided what to do about the 15.0 x 8.6 x 2.9 inch line on a 12-inch title.

Care is not a reason to pick one. Both tell you to preheat on low to moderate heat, dry food before it goes in, and let stainless release before you turn it. Both warn that salt in a cold pan can pit the inside, and that a hot pan should not go under cold water. The limited lifetime warranty covers All-Clad cookware on both pages. Read that warranty text on the pan you order. The D3 model page is the 12-inch fry pan already listed here, not a saucepan and not an 8-inch skillet.

Questions

Is All-Clad D5 the same metal stack as D3?

No. The D3 page says tri-ply construction that combines five metal layers into three functional layers. The D5 page says 5-ply bonded construction.

Are both 12-inch pans oven safe to the same temperature?

Yes. Both pages say oven and broiler safe up to 600 F, and both say heat over 500 F can discolor stainless without changing performance.

Can both go on induction?

Yes. Both pages say the pan is compatible with any stovetop, including induction.

Why are the dimension lines not in the table?

The D3 12-inch page prints 20.1 x 12.5 x 4.4 inches. The D5 12-inch page prints 15.0 x 8.6 x 2.9 inches. Those official lines do not describe the same footprint, so they are not set side by side as if they were.

What models and warranties are these?

D3 is model 4112. D5 is model SD55112. Both pages say a limited lifetime warranty, hand washing, and a polished stainless finish. The weight lines print 3.000000 and 4.000000 with no unit named.
