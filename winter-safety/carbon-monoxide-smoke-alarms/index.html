Carbon monoxide and smoke alarms

Certification marks to read on a CO or smoke alarm, including UL 2034 and CSA 6.19, plus the official recall search and two notice patterns.

Space heatersElectric blanketsCO and smoke alarmsOutage power

What to read on the label

  • The CPSC voluntary-standards page names UL 2034, 4th Edition, Single and Multiple Station Carbon Monoxide Alarms as the standard for carbon monoxide alarms. Read the alarm label for UL 2034.
  • Natural Resources Canada says to buy a CO alarm certified to the current CSA 6.19 Residential Carbon Monoxide Alarming Devices standard. Health Canada says the certification mark has to be to Canadian safety standards, on the alarm and on the packaging, and the mark description on that page includes CSA 6.19 with a year.
  • For a smoke alarm in Canada, read the label for a Canadian certification mark. The Health Canada notice below says the Residential Detectors Regulations require smoke alarms to meet the applicable Canadian standards.

How to search a recall

For a purchase in the United States, search the brand and the model on CPSC Recalls. For a purchase in Canada, search the same words on Health Canada recalls and safety alerts. Open the notice and match the model number on the unit. A family name on the box can point at a different model.

The catalogue has no smoke alarm card and no carbon monoxide alarm card. Search the model number on the back of the unit.

Patterns in official notices

Each pattern below is one official notice. The sentence is what that notice says.

  • CPSC: Kidde Recalls Combination Smoke/CO Alarms Due to Alarm Failure. The notice says model KN-COSM-IB, with manufacture dates from June 1, 2004 through December 31, 2010, can fail to keep chirping at its 7-year end of life if the batteries are replaced. It says people can then believe the alarm is still working.
  • Health Canada: Dual-Function Smoke and Carbon Monoxide Alarm recalled due to potential failure to operate. The notice says model WJ-SC05 does not have a Canadian certification mark, and that it is unknown whether the alarm operates in the presence of smoke. It says to stop using it and replace it with one that bears a Canadian certification mark. The model number is on the label on the back.

Questions

Which CO alarm standard does the CPSC page name?

The CPSC voluntary-standards page names UL 2034, 4th Edition, Single and Multiple Station Carbon Monoxide Alarms. Read the alarm label for that standard.

What does Health Canada say to look for on a CO alarm?

Health Canada says to look for a certification mark to Canadian safety standards on the alarm and on the packaging. Natural Resources Canada names CSA 6.19, Residential Carbon Monoxide Alarming Devices, as the standard to buy to.

Where do I search an alarm recall?

Search the brand and the model number from the back label on CPSC Recalls for a US purchase and on Health Canada recalls and safety alerts for a Canadian purchase.
