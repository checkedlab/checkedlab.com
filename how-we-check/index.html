How we check

Checked Lab lines up maker pages, store links, and public recall records for shoppers in the US and Canada.

What we check

  • Manufacturer pages, and only the sentences those pages actually state. The link sits next to the sentence.
  • Store buttons for the region you are in. US buttons go to Amazon.com, Best Buy, Walmart, Target, and Home Depot. Canada buttons go to Amazon.ca, Best Buy, Canadian Tire, Walmart, and Wayfair. The button opens that store’s search for the brand and model, or the Amazon product page when the ASIN is already verified.
  • US recalls from the CPSC SaferProducts API, and Canadian recalls from Health Canada’s open dataset d38de914-c94c-429b-8ab1-8776c31643e3. A daily GitHub Action reruns that match.

How a card is built

  • Recall-checked means the CPSC and Health Canada feeds were matched to the brand and model.
  • Prices stay on the store sites. The buttons open the store.
  • Placement on a card is editorial. Brands do not buy a slot on the page.

Prices

Each card says prices change often, so check the current price at the store. A confirmed product page opens when that marketplace’s ASIN is known. Otherwise Amazon opens a search for the brand and model. Other stores open their own search for the same words. The links are sponsored and open in a new tab.

Recalls

Official recall found means the brand and the model token both appear in one recall record. Other recalls that mention this brand name can appear under the card. A Check model badge means the model still needs that match.

“No recall found as of {date}” means the check ran on that date and no record matched this model. It is not a promise that a recall can never exist.

Region

The US / CA switch starts from a choice saved in this browser. If you have not chosen, a Canadian time zone selects Canada even when the browser language is set to the US. Otherwise the browser language is used, and the US is the fallback. Winter copy follows the region: snow-belt states in the US, and a Canadian winter in Canada.
