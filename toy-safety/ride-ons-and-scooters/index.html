Ride-ons and scooters

Helmet marks, the Canadian advice on e-scooters, and what UL 2272 covers on a hoverboard or e-scooter.

What to read on the label

  • On a hoverboard or other personal e-mobility device, look for a certification mark for the whole unit. The CPSC hoverboard alert says a mark on the packaging and on the product is the indication of UL 2272 compliance, and that a certified battery or charger alone does not make the hoverboard UL 2272 compliant.
  • UL 2272 says the standard covers the electrical drive train, including the battery system and other circuitry, for energy and electrical shock hazards. It says the standard does not evaluate performance, reliability, or the physical hazards of use.
  • For a scooter, read the helmet type. Health Canada’s back-to-school page lists a bicycle helmet for electric and non-electric scooters, and says to look for a certification label such as CSA, ASTM, EN, or SNELL. CPSC bicycle-helmet guidance says the US requirements are in 16 CFR part 1203.
  • Health Canada’s ride-on advice says falls are the most common injuries with ride-on toys, tricycles, scooters, and wagons, and says to use helmets and protective gear when recommended, especially for scooters. It also says the Canadian Paediatric Society advises that e-scooters and e-bikes are not safe for kids under 16 to drive or to ride as passengers.

How to search a recall

For a purchase in the United States, search the brand and the model on CPSC Recalls. For a purchase in Canada, search the same words on Health Canada recalls and safety alerts. Open the notice and match the model number on the unit. A family name on the box can point at a different model.

A kick scooter and an e-scooter are different products. Read the listing for a battery. If it has one, search that model and read the battery mark before you treat it as a push scooter.

Patterns in official notices

Each pattern below is one official page. The sentence is what that page says.

  • CPSC: Hoverboard Safety Alert. The alert says CPSC is aware of more than 300 self-balancing scooter incidents related to fires or overheating, and it describes a March 2017 house fire in Harrisburg, Pennsylvania, in which two children died. It says to use only the charger supplied with the hoverboard, to charge it while someone is watching, and to keep it away from flammable items. It says hoverboards should comply with UL 2272, and that compliance cannot guarantee a hoverboard will not overheat or catch fire. It says a hoverboard bought before January 29, 2016 is not UL 2272 compliant.
  • UL: UL 2272, Electrical Systems for Personal E-Mobility Devices. The UL page lists ANSI/CAN/UL 2272 with a published date of April 19, 2024, and a revision dated March 23, 2026. The scope on that page says the requirements cover the electrical drive train system, including the battery system, for electric scooters and other personal e-mobility devices. It says the standard evaluates the drive train and the battery and charger combination for energy and electrical shock hazards, and does not evaluate physical hazards of use.
  • Health Canada: Lithium-ion batteries in e-mobility devices. The advisory says Health Canada and Transport Canada recommend that the e-mobility device and its battery comply with ANSI/CAN/UL 2272 and ANSI/CAN/UL 2849. It says to use the lithium-ion battery that came with the e-bike or e-scooter, to replace it only with original parts, and not to build, modify, or add an external battery to raise voltage or capacity.
  • CPSC: Bicycle helmets, 16 CFR part 1203. The page says 16 CFR part 1203 sets performance requirements for bicycle helmets. It says helmets are tested so they do not block vision, do not come off in a fall, keep their straps from stretching enough to release, and reduce the force to the head. It says a helmet marketed only for skateboarding, in-line skating, baseball, or roller hockey is not covered by the bicycle-helmet requirements.
  • Health Canada: Helmets for scooters. The page’s helmet table lists a bicycle helmet, described as single-impact, for scooters, electric scooters, and non-electric scooters. It says to look on the helmet for a certification label such as CSA, ASTM, EN, or SNELL, and to avoid a used helmet when the history is unknown or the helmet looks damaged.

Questions

What does a UL 2272 mark cover?

The CPSC hoverboard alert says the certification mark should be for the whole self-balancing scooter or personal e-mobility device, not only a battery pack or charger. The UL 2272 page says the standard evaluates the electrical drive train and the battery and charger combination for energy and electrical shock hazards, and does not evaluate the physical hazards of riding.

Which helmet page applies to a scooter?

Health Canada’s back-to-school table lists a bicycle helmet for electric and non-electric scooters. CPSC says bicycle helmets sold in the United States are covered by 16 CFR part 1203. Health Canada says to look for a certification label such as CSA, ASTM, EN, or SNELL.

What does Health Canada say about children on e-scooters?

Health Canada’s ride-on page says the Canadian Paediatric Society advises that e-scooters and e-bikes are not safe for kids under 16 to drive or to ride as passengers. The lithium-ion advisory says to keep the original battery and not to modify it, and recommends ANSI/CAN/UL 2272 for the device and its battery.
