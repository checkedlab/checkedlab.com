ThermoWorks Thermapen

Thermometer

ThermoWorks Thermapen

On this list because a ThermoWorks Thermapen is a widely sold instant-read food thermometer, and USDA FSIS says to check a turkey with a food thermometer.

Read the model on the thermometer. USDA Turkey Basics: Safe Cooking says to use a food thermometer and to reach 165 °F in the innermost part of the thigh and wing and the thickest part of the breast. Let’s Talk Turkey says a pop-up indicator is still checked with a food thermometer in those places. The September 3, 2014 CPSC probe notice names iDevices Pro probes made from May 2014 through June 2014, not every thermometer. More is on the turkey temperatures guide. Search the model on CPSC Recalls and on Health Canada recalls and safety alerts.

Roasting pan with a wire rack

Roasting pan

Roasting pan with a wire rack

On this list because USDA FSIS says to set a turkey breast-side up on a flat wire rack in a shallow roasting pan.

Read the material and the depth on the pan. USDA Turkey Basics: Safe Cooking says to use a shallow roasting pan 2 to 2 1/2 inches deep, and says a dark roasting pan cooks faster than a shiny one. It still says doneness is 165 °F on a food thermometer. Search the brand and model on CPSC Recalls and on Health Canada recalls and safety alerts.

Wusthof carving set

Knife

Wusthof carving set

On this list because USDA FSIS says to let the turkey stand 20 minutes before carving, and a carving set is sold for that step.

Read the model on the knife or the box. USDA Turkey Basics: Safe Cooking says the 20-minute stand is so the juices set and the turkey carves more easily. The catalogue card for the Wusthof Classic 8-inch cook’s knife is a different product from a carving set. Search the carving-set model on CPSC Recalls and on Health Canada recalls and safety alerts.

Le Creuset Dutch oven

Dutch oven

Le Creuset Dutch oven

On this list because a Le Creuset Dutch oven is a widely sold lidded pot, and the CPSC holiday cooking release says to smother a grease fire by sliding a lid over the pan.

Read the size on the pot. The November 19, 2025 CPSC holiday cooking release says to slide a lid over a grease fire and turn off the burner, and never to use water. The NFPA desk reference says not to move the pan and to leave the lid on until the pan has cooled. The catalogue card for the Le Creuset Signature 5.5-quart Dutch oven is one model. A clear line on that card does not cover a different size. Search the size you are buying on CPSC Recalls and on Health Canada recalls and safety alerts.

OXO gravy separator

Kitchen tool

OXO gravy separator

On this list because an OXO gravy separator is a widely sold tool for separating fat from pan drippings, and USDA FSIS gives a storage time for gravy.

Read the model on the separator. Let’s Talk Turkey says to discard gravy left out longer than 2 hours, or 1 hour above 90 °F, and to use refrigerated gravy within 3 to 4 days. It says reheated turkey should reach 165 °F. Search the model on CPSC Recalls and on Health Canada recalls and safety alerts.

Kidde kitchen fire extinguisher

Fire extinguisher

Kidde kitchen fire extinguisher

On this list because a Kidde fire extinguisher is a widely sold home extinguisher, and the CPSC holiday cooking release says to have a fire extinguisher nearby and know how to use it.

Read the model number, the date code, and the classification label. USFA’s extinguisher page says to look for a UL Listed or ULC Listed label, describes Class B for grease and other flammable liquids, Class K for cooking oils and fats, and multipurpose A-B-C extinguishers for most home fires. The November 2, 2017 CPSC Kidde notice names plastic-handle models made from January 1, 1973 through August 15, 2017, and push-button Pindicator extinguishers. It counts units sold in Canada. More is on the kitchen fires guide. Search the model on CPSC Recalls and on Health Canada recalls and safety alerts.
